# 3. Changing the looper

The looper is now two presets, C1 and D6. Changing it by hand would mean
making every change twice, and the day you forget, the copies drift apart.
In this app you change the *state*, and the copies follow.

Carry on from [tutorial 2](2-a-second-way-in.md).

## Edit once

In **Program**, choose **D6** (or C1 — either is the looper). Three things
say you're editing more than one preset:

The context bar offers **Changes: All 2 presets**, and it's chosen:

![Changes: All 2 presets / D6 only](../images/t3-scope.png)

The display shows the number of presets in place of the preset number:

![The display: LOOPR ×2](../images/t3-display.png)

And the preset panel below names them: *D6 is one of 2 presets that behave
alike — "LOOPR" on the map (C1, D6).*

Now make a change: tap switch **6** and set its colours to **10A Lavender** /
**10B Dim Lavender**. Then choose C1: its switch 6 is lavender too. Change
anything — a CC, a step, the name, the MIDI sent on load — and both copies
get it.

One thing stays per preset: **where a jump leads**. Switch A's target is
what tells the copies apart, so it's edited on the copy in front of you
only. Change routes on the map instead, which keeps every copy's way back
right.

## See the copies

The [preset chooser](../reference/the-faceplate.md#the-preset-chooser) has a
**By state** view that groups them:

![The preset chooser by state](../images/t3-chooser.png)

## Editing one copy on purpose

Choosing **D6 only** sends your edits to D6 alone. The moment it differs
from C1 in anything but its jumps, it's no longer the same state: the map
shows two looper boxes, and D6's A is just a jump to A2. That's sometimes
what you want — a looper variant for one song — but it's a different design,
so the choice is never made for you.

## Tidy the slots

Copies land wherever there was room. To put them together — say, the whole
looper in bank D — click LOOPR on the map, and use **Move to…** on a copy:
the switches that lead to it follow it to its new slot. Only empty presets
are offered, so clear one first.

That's the whole idea: you design *a looper that comes back*, and the app
keeps the presets that implement it in step.
